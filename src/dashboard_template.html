How to read this, and what it cannot tell you
  • The number is a share, not a count. "62%" means about 62 of every 100 cases expected in that zone may never reach the reports. It says nothing about how many cases there are.
  • The range matters more than the dot. A wide range means the signals are weak or mixed. Treat it as a reason to check, not a verdict.
  • Flags describe gaps in the response, not blame on communities. "Community" means contacts are not being reached or burials are being refused. It is a prompt for engagement, not for enforcement.
  • Reports from the last two weeks are still arriving, so the newest numbers are incomplete. The tool uses a four-week average to soften this.
  • Tested only on invented outbreaks. In 20 simulated outbreaks the transparent score ranked zones well and its 80% ranges held 85% of the time. Real data will behave differently, and that is what the project will find out.
  • Ranking zones by case counts is still better for "where are most cases". This tool answers a different question: where are cases being missed.
Method: a transparent weighted score of routine warning signals (reports received, report delay, contacts followed up, cases from known contacts, deaths in the community, burial refusals, security incidents, community workers with a phone), with ranges adjusted on recent errors. Cause = which group of signals looks worse than the typical zone that week.
